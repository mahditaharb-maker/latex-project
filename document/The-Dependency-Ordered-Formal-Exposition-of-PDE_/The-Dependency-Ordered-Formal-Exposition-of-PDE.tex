	\begin{abstract}
		\bfseries
		\setstretch{1.3}{\small This document is a step-indexed,
			dependency-ordered presentation of the theory of partial differential
			equations, following \cite{brezis2011, evans2010} . Every result
			appears only after all results it depends on. Entries are split into
			postulates (unproved statements used inside proofs) and axioms of the
			ordered field $\R$ (primitive structure). The document covers the full
			prerequisite tower (measure theory, $L^{p}$ spaces, Hilbert and Banach
			space theory, convex analysis, integration by parts) and the PDE solution
			layer built on it: Sobolev spaces and weak derivatives, existence,
			uniqueness and regularity for the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian via
			Lax--Milgram, the weak maximum principle and Hopf lemma, the Dirichlet
			principle, weak existence and energy estimates for the heat and wave
			equations, and Duhamel's principle. Each entry is annotated with the
			choice strength of its proof: \textsf{ZF}, \textsf{ZF+DC}, or \textsf{ZFC}.}
	\end{abstract}

	\section{Introduction}
	% ============================================================
	\noindent The document is organised into six parts: \emph{Axioms},
	\emph{Postulates}, \emph{Definitions}, \emph{Statements} (Lemmas,
	Theorems), \emph{Proofs}, and \emph{Scripts}. Following the
	reverse-mathematics convention of \cite{simpson2009}, entries are split
	into two primitive layers: \emph{axioms of the ordered field $\R$} and
	\emph{postulates} (unproved statements admitted inside proofs). Every
	entry in a later part may depend only on earlier entries, and no entry
	may depend on anything outside the union of axioms and postulates.
	Lemmas and Theorems form a single dependency layer: an entry may depend
	on an earlier Lemma or Theorem, so long as the global order is respected.
	
	Choice-strength tags record the strength needed to make the given proof
	go through; they are upper bounds on the logical strength of each entry
	and are not claimed to be tight.

	\section{Scripts}
	
	\label{sec:scripts}
	
	\noindent Each tree records, for one result, the entries used by its proof,
	transitively closed. Names coincide with the names that appear in the
	statement tables. Leaves are either postulates (unproved statements admitted
	inside proofs), axioms of the ordered field $\R$ (primitive structure), or
	primitive definitions. In the trees below the prefix \verb|Ld | carries the
	depth of the node (root at \verb|L0|) and the name is indented \verb|2*d|
	spaces after the pipe, so every chain can be read straight down a column of
	increasing depth labels. Trees are typeset in a two-column grid, each tree
	inside its own \verb|minipage| so that the depth-ordered chains can be
	compared side by side.

	\section{Conclusion}
\setstretch{2}
	\noindent The document presents the dependency-ordered exposition of PDE
	theory (prerequisites plus the solution layer). All entries are organised into six parts (Axioms, Postulates,
	Definitions, Statements, Proofs, Scripts) and the choice strength of
	every result is recorded in the class column as \textsf{ZF},
	\textsf{ZF+DC}, or \textsf{ZFC}. Within the Statements part, Lemmas and
	Theorems form a single dependency layer, so long as each entry appears
	only after all entries it depends on. In particular \textsf{Riesz} is
	stated among the Lemmas because \textsf{LaxMilgram} depends on it,
	\textsf{LaxMilgram} precedes \textsf{BoundsSpectrum} for the same
	reason, \textsf{WaveEnergyConservation} precedes \textsf{WaveWeakExistence},
	and \textsf{RellichKondrachov} is stated among the Theorems (after
	\textsf{WeakCompact}) because its proof uses the weak compactness
	principle.
	
	Two closure properties are worth flagging. First, the boundary
	regularity of the Dirichlet problem is admitted as a postulate
	(\textsf{EllipticBoundaryReg-Step}) rather than derived from the interior
	regularity theorem, because the latter only yields
	$H^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Second, the heat semigroup $S(t)$ used in the
	proof of Duhamel's principle is introduced through the postulate
	\textsf{HeatSemigroupGenerator}, which records the generator identity
	$S^{\prime}(t)=\Delta S(t)$ as an external input rather than deriving it
	from the Galerkin construction. Both postulates are tagged
	\textsf{ZF+DC} and are the only assumptions of their respective proofs
	that are not established elsewhere in the document.
	
	Every lemma and theorem stated in the Statements section has a
	corresponding proof table in the Proofs section, and the proofs are
	presented in exactly the same order as the statements.
